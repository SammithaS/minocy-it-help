# Battery draining fast, not charging, or swollen

Use this when your battery runs out quickly, won't charge, or the laptop case looks bulged.

**Safety first:** if the laptop case is bulging, the trackpad is pushed up, the keyboard is lifting, or you smell burning — **stop using it, unplug it, switch it off and keep it away from heat and anything flammable.** Don't press on it or try to open it. Raise a CRITICAL ticket right away.

## Symptoms

- The battery goes from full to empty in a couple of hours
- "Plugged in, not charging" or the charging light doesn't come on
- The battery percentage jumps around or the laptop dies suddenly
- The case, trackpad or keyboard is raised or bulging

## Quick fixes

1. Check the charger is the {{company}}-issued one and is firmly plugged in at both ends. Try a different wall socket.
2. Look at the cable and plug for damage. Stop using a charger with a frayed cable or bent pins.
3. Turn on battery saver (Windows: Settings → System → Power & battery; Mac: System Settings → Battery → Low Power Mode) and lower screen brightness.
4. Close apps that use a lot of power — video calls, many browser tabs and games drain the battery fastest.
5. Restart the laptop with the charger connected.

## Advanced steps

1. Check battery health: on Mac, System Settings → Battery → Battery Health; on Windows, ask IT for a battery report if the laptop is more than two years old.
2. Try another {{company}} charger of the same type, for example a colleague's, to see if the charger is the problem.

## Escalate when

- The battery or case is swollen, the laptop smells of burning, or it's too hot to touch — raise as CRITICAL and stop using it
- The laptop won't charge with a known-good charger
- Battery health shows "Service recommended" or capacity is below 70%
- The charger or cable is damaged and you need a replacement
